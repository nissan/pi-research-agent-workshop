# Research Brief — Generic Run

## Executive summary

A grant-opportunity triage agent appears to be a promising repeatable workflow for a boutique consulting firm, especially if it can reduce manual scanning time and produce consistent first-pass recommendations. The strongest case is operational efficiency: the same eligibility, deadline, fit, and document checks recur across clients. However, the current evidence is source-note-only and synthetic, so a real pilot should test time saved, accuracy, and consultant trust.

## Key findings

1. **Fact — The workflow is repeatable.** Consultants repeatedly check deadlines, eligibility, funding amount, sector fit, geography, required documents, and likelihood of success.
2. **Fact — The current process has consistency problems.** Research quality varies depending on who performs the scan.
3. **Fact — Clients want recommendations, not link dumps.** This favors an agent that summarizes, ranks, and recommends next action.
4. **Assumption — Time savings could justify payment.** The notes estimate 1–3 hours saved per client scan, but this needs validation.
5. **Recommendation — Start with decision support, not autonomous final judgment.** Grant criteria can change and legal/accounting implications may require human review.

## Evidence notes

Evidence comes from `sources/source-notes.md`. The notes describe pain points, candidate value, risks, and a monetization hypothesis, but do not include external market data or measured time trials.

## Evidence quality

Medium-low. The workflow logic is plausible, but the sources are synthetic classroom notes. Use this as a pilot hypothesis, not proof of market demand.

## Risks and uncertainties

- Grant criteria may change after the agent scans them.
- Eligibility requirements may be misread if source quality is weak.
- Consultants may not trust recommendations without transparent scoring.
- The agent could create false confidence if assumptions are not labelled.

## Recommended next action

Run a 5-client pilot: compare manual triage against agent-assisted triage, measure time saved, missed eligibility issues, consultant edits, and client usefulness.

## Open questions

- Which grant portals and document types matter most?
- What level of scoring transparency would consultants trust?
- What price is justified by verified time savings?
- How often must sources be refreshed?

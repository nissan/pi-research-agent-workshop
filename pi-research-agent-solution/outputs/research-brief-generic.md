# Research brief: Specialized AI research agent for grant-opportunity triage

## 1. Executive summary

**Decision question:** Is grant-opportunity triage repeatable enough for a boutique consulting firm to package as a specialist agent that other agents or operators would pay to use?

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a small pilot, not a full product build yet. The workflow appears repeatable because consultants repeatedly check the same categories across grant opportunities: deadline, eligibility, funding amount, sector fit, geography, required documents, and likelihood of success. The strongest value case is saving 1–3 hours per client scan while improving consistency and producing concise recommendations. However, source quality and changing grant criteria create risk, so the agent should be positioned as a triage and evidence-packaging tool with required human review for legal, accounting, and final eligibility judgments.

## 2. Key findings

1. **Fact — The workflow has repeatable inputs and evaluation steps.** Consultants already scan similar sources and assess recurring fields such as deadline, eligibility, funding amount, sector fit, geography, required documents, and likelihood of success.

2. **Fact — Current pain points are well matched to agent support.** Manual tracking can miss deadlines, eligibility details are easy to misread, consultants spend time on clearly irrelevant opportunities, and research quality varies by staff member.

3. **Fact — The proposed agent output maps to client needs.** Clients want concise recommendations rather than link dumps; the candidate agent would produce scores, a shortlist, next actions, document requirements, and a proof bundle.

4. **Assumption — Buyers may pay if time savings and consistency are proven.** The monetization hypothesis depends on the agent saving consultants 1–3 hours per client scan and improving consistency. This is plausible from the notes but not yet validated by buyer data.

5. **Recommendation — Build around decision support, not autonomous grant advice.** Because grant criteria can change and public pages may be incomplete, the agent should flag evidence quality, cite source notes, timestamp outputs, and route legal/accounting claims and final eligibility calls to humans.

6. **Recommendation — Make trust artifacts part of the product.** The proof bundle — source list, timestamp, scoring rubric, and output hash — should be treated as a core feature because poor source quality can create false confidence.

## 3. Evidence notes

- **Current workflow evidence:** Consultants manually scan grant portals, newsletters, incubator announcements, and government sites.
- **Repeated task evidence:** The team often repeats the same first-pass triage across similar clients.
- **Assessment criteria evidence:** Each opportunity is checked for deadline, eligibility, funding amount, sector fit, geography, required documents, and likelihood of success.
- **Pain point evidence:** Deadlines are missed, eligibility is easy to misread, irrelevant opportunities waste time, junior staff struggle with strategic fit, and clients want concise recommendations.
- **Candidate value evidence:** The agent could accept a company profile and list of opportunities, score eligibility/fit/urgency/evidence quality, create a shortlist, identify next actions and document requirements, and produce a proof bundle.
- **Evidence quality:** Source notes are synthetic classroom-safe notes, not market research or customer interviews. They are sufficient for a preliminary product hypothesis but weak for pricing, demand validation, or claims about actual willingness to pay.

## 4. Risks and uncertainties

- **Changing criteria:** Grant criteria can change after the agent reviews an opportunity.
- **Incomplete sources:** Public pages may omit important requirements or updates.
- **False confidence:** Poor source quality could make a scored recommendation look more certain than it is.
- **Human review required:** Legal/accounting claims and final eligibility interpretations require human review.
- **Unproven monetization:** The 1–3 hour savings and willingness-to-pay hypothesis has not been validated with actual users or buyers.

## 5. Recommended next action

Run a limited pilot on several real or representative client scans. Measure whether the agent reduces first-pass triage time by 1–3 hours, improves consistency across consultants, correctly flags weak evidence, and produces shortlists that senior consultants consider decision-ready. Use the pilot to decide whether to package the agent for broader use.

## 6. Open questions

1. How often does the consulting team perform grant-opportunity scans per month?
2. What is the current average time spent per first-pass scan?
3. What level of accuracy or consistency would make senior consultants trust the agent?
4. Which source types are most reliable for grant criteria and deadline updates?
5. What price would be justified if the agent reliably saved 1–3 hours per scan?
6. Who is the primary buyer: the consulting firm itself, other consultants, startup operators, or other agents in a workflow?

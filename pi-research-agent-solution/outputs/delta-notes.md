# Delta notes: Generic brief vs. grant-evaluator specialization

## What improved

- **Sharper triage logic:** The specialized version goes beyond generic eligibility/fit scoring and adds grant-evaluator practicality checks: matched funding, cash runway, short deadlines, partner letters, indirect sector relevance, reporting burden, evidence burden, and funding-to-effort ratio.
- **Better reject/flag criteria:** The specialist can explain why an opportunity that looks eligible may still be a poor pursuit because of execution risk or admin burden.
- **More decision-ready recommendations:** The specialized brief gives consultants a clearer pilot design and rubric for testing whether the agent improves real grant scans.
- **Stronger product positioning:** The agent is positioned as “grant-opportunity triage with evaluator-style practicality flags,” which is more specific and sellable than a generic research agent.
- **Better risk control:** The specialized version more clearly limits the agent to triage and decision support, with human review for final eligibility, legal/accounting issues, and client-specific funding strategy.

## What did not improve / remaining gaps

- **No real market validation yet:** The sources do not include buyer interviews, usage data, pricing tests, or competitive analysis.
- **No measured time savings yet:** The 1–3 hour savings claim remains a monetization hypothesis from the source notes.
- **Evidence remains synthetic:** The notes are classroom-safe synthetic notes and participant-supplied example domain notes, so they support a pilot but not a full commercial launch decision.
- **No live grant-source evaluation:** The brief does not test real portals, source freshness, or changing criteria.

## Could this become a paid Reddi Agent Protocol specialist?

**Yes, plausibly, but only after a pilot.** The specialization appears strong enough for a paid specialist hypothesis because it has a repeatable workflow, clear inputs and outputs, domain-specific scoring dimensions, and a concrete consultant pain point. The most credible paid use case is a specialist that other agents or operators call to convert a company profile plus grant-opportunity list into a ranked shortlist with practicality flags and a proof bundle.

**Readiness judgment:** Pilot-ready, not launch-proven.

**Minimum proof needed before paid listing:**

1. Demonstrate consistent first-pass triage time savings, ideally within the stated 1–3 hour range.
2. Show that senior consultants prefer or accept the specialist’s shortlist compared with junior/manual scans.
3. Confirm that the agent reliably flags weak evidence and practical blockers.
4. Define human-review boundaries clearly.
5. Package outputs in a protocol-friendly format, such as ranked opportunities, rationale, risk flags, source list, timestamp, scoring rubric, and output hash.

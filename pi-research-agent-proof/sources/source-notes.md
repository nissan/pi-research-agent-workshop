# Source notes — grant-opportunity triage agent

These are classroom-safe synthetic notes for the exercise.

## Current workflow

- Consultants manually scan grant portals, newsletters, incubator announcements, and government sites.
- Each opportunity is checked for deadline, eligibility, funding amount, sector fit, geography, required documents, and likelihood of success.
- The team often repeats the same first-pass triage across similar clients.
- Research quality varies depending on who performs the scan.

## Pain points

- Deadlines are missed when opportunities are tracked manually.
- Eligibility details are easy to misread.
- Consultants waste time reading opportunities that are clearly irrelevant.
- Junior staff can summarize opportunities but struggle to judge strategic fit.
- Clients want concise recommendations, not a dump of links.

## Candidate specialist-agent value

- Accepts a company profile and a list of opportunities.
- Scores each opportunity for eligibility, fit, urgency, and evidence quality.
- Produces a shortlist with next action and document requirements.
- Creates a proof bundle: source list, timestamp, scoring rubric, and output hash.

## Risks

- Grant criteria can change.
- Public pages may be incomplete.
- Legal/accounting claims require human review.
- Poor source quality can create false confidence.

## Monetization hypothesis

A specialized grant-triage agent may be worth paying for if it saves consultants 1–3 hours per client scan and improves consistency.
